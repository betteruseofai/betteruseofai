# Code of conduct

> Status: draft. This copy hasn't been through a voice review yet.

This is a small project about being honest with numbers. The same standard applies to how people
here treat each other. It is modelled on the
[Contributor Covenant, version 2.1](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/version/2/1/code_of_conduct/),
rewritten in this project's voice and shortened. Where this page is unclear, the Covenant is the
longer reference.

## What we expect

Anyone taking part in this project, in an issue, a pull request, a review, a discussion or
anywhere else that represents it, is expected to:

- Be direct and be kind. Say what is wrong with a change, and say it about the change.
- Assume the other person is trying. Most disagreements here are about where a boundary was drawn,
  and both sides are usually counting different things.
- Accept a correction the way you would want yours accepted. Being wrong about a number is normal
  here; refusing to look at the source is the problem.
- Respect people's privacy. Do not publish someone's private information, and do not dig for it.
- Stay on the work. This is a repository about the resource cost of language models, and that is
  what it is for.

## What we do not accept

- Harassment of any kind, in public or in private.
- Insults, slurs or demeaning remarks about a person, whether about who they are or how they work.
- Sexualised language or attention.
- Sustained disruption of a discussion, or reopening one that has been closed with a decision.
- Threats, or encouraging others to do any of the above.

## Reporting

Write to the maintainer at the address on the GitHub profile of `gordianknot-legacy`, with the
subject line starting `conduct:`. Say what happened, where it happened, when, and who was involved, and include links where you
can. Reports are read by the maintainer only, and are not shared further without your agreement,
except where the law requires it.

We reply within a week, and we say what we are going to do.

## What happens

The maintainer decides, in proportion to what happened and whether it has happened before:

1. **A private word.** For a first lapse, an explanation of what was wrong and what we expect.
2. **A public correction.** Where the behaviour was public and a correction is owed to the people who
   saw it.
3. **A pause.** No interaction with the project for a stated period.
4. **Removal.** For sustained or severe behaviour, no further participation.

Anyone the decision affects can ask for it to be reconsidered once, in writing, and will get an
answer.

## Scope

This applies wherever somebody is representing the project: the repository, its issues and pull
requests, any discussion space it runs, and anywhere a person speaks for it. It does not follow
people into their own spaces.
