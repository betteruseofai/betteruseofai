# Voice

> Status: draft. This is the voice model the site copy is written against. It comes from writing
> samples supplied on 2026-09-11. Every page still needs reading by Anirudh before its `status`
> moves past `draft`.

`STYLE.md` says what we must not write. This says what we're trying to sound like.

## What the samples are

Four texts arrived. They are not equally useful, and pretending otherwise would produce a worse
site, so here is what we're using and what we're not.

| Sample | What it is | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| "The Incessant Snarl-Up", a first-person piece about being stuck at Silk Board junction | Personal writing, unmediated | **Primary** |
| A short travel note ("I am a sucker for weekend travel") | Personal writing, unmediated | **Primary** |
| A listicle about EV charging networks | Commercial content writing, written to a client brief | Discounted |
| A long piece on Indian secularism and the Congress Party | Published academic analysis, not Anirudh's writing | Not used as a voice model |

The EV listicle is discounted on purpose. It does the things `STYLE.md` bans, because that is what
the brief asked for: "seamlessly navigate", "numero uno", "the writing on the wall", exclamation
marks, "Read on to find out". That is house style for paid content work, not a personal voice.

The secularism piece is a scholarly article. We took one habit from it, described below, and
nothing else.

## What carries over

**Concrete before abstract, always.** The snarl-up piece never says the traffic was bad. It says a
two-foot gap, twenty-five minutes, a man in his late sixties, a JCB and a minivan. The site works
the same way: not "significant energy use" but 1.08 Wh, and a link to where that came from.

**Numbers are specific and often spelled out in prose.** "About twenty minutes", "one kilometre
left", "about 20 seconds". In running copy we write small numbers as words. In a readout they are
always digits, in the mono face, with their range.

**Deadpan.** The humour in the sample is in the situation, never in the adjective. The narrator
reports a countdown treated like a Mars launch and adds nothing. Our equivalent is reporting that
one published estimate is forty times another and leaving it there.

**Running observation, not argument.** The piece recalculates as it goes: quarter to seven, still
a kilometre, might make it. The reader watches someone think. On the methodology pages we show the
working rather than presenting a conclusion.

**Honest about the outcome.** The narrator misses the play and books a ticket for the next day. No
lesson is drawn. When our estimate is weak we say it is weak and move on.

**Indian and British spelling throughout.** Kilometre, metres, realise, litre. Already the rule.

**Qualify rather than overstate.** This is the one thing taken from the secularism piece: it keeps
writing "needs to be qualified in at least two other ways" and "it may be premature to conclude".
That is exactly the posture for a project whose whole subject is uncertainty.

## What does not carry over

The samples are first person singular, and often past tense. The site is "we" and present tense.
What transfers is the register, not the grammar.

The samples also run long sentences chained with commas. On the site, sentences average under
twenty words. A long one is fine occasionally, and it should be doing something.

## A worked comparison

Not the voice:

> Our cutting-edge methodology leverages peer-reviewed benchmarks to empower users with
> unprecedented visibility into their AI footprint.

Closer:

> We take the published measurements, which disagree with each other by a factor of forty, and show
> you the whole spread rather than picking a favourite.

Not the voice:

> Did you know a single prompt uses water? It's not just electricity, it's cooling too.

Closer:

> Data centres use water to stay cool. Google puts a median text prompt at 0.26 mL on site. A
> lifecycle figure from Mistral for one of its own models is nearer 45 mL. Both are defensible,
> because they are counting different things.
